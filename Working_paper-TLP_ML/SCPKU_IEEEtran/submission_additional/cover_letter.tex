Please consider the enclosed research article, \textbf{``Proxy-Based Urban Change Prediction under Spatial Generalization: Improving Tail Reliability with Lightweight Calibration,''} for publication in \textit{Transactions in GIS}.

\vspace{0.5em}

This study addresses a critical methodology gap in remote-sensing-enabled urban monitoring: the severe mismatch between reported model performance and real-world reliability when predictions are transferred to unseen spatial units. Using the CivitasGrid-TLP dataset (2015--2024), we evaluate proxy-based urban change prediction from WorldPop and VIIRS under a leakage-aware GroupSplit(grid\_id) protocol. We benchmark multiple machine learning regressors and introduce a lightweight post-hoc calibration method tailored for spatial regression.

\vspace{0.5em}

Our main findings deliver key insights for the GIScience community:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $\Delta$WorldPop is substantially more predictable than $\Delta$VIIRS, whose year-to-year differencing amplifies observational noise and leaves weak signal under spatial generalization.
    \item Tail errors (rare local shocks) dominate practical model failure, meaning that conventional average metrics (e.g., global $R^2$) severely understate forecasting risks in vulnerable areas.
    \item Our proposed RMSE-anchored contrastive affine calibrator consistently improves tail reliability across diverse baselines, providing an effective, model-agnostic tool to safeguard spatial predictions against long-tailed anomalies.
\end{itemize}
